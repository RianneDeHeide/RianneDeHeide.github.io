Finding your way in the Dutch research community

The structure of Dutch academic research can be rather confusing, even for me. I did my bachelor's, master's and PhD in the Netherlands, speak Dutch fluently, and serve on committees at both NWO and Platform Wiskunde Nederland (PWN). Yet it was only while writing this page that I properly worked out how all the different organisations fit together. I wrote it mainly for researchers starting a faculty position in the Netherlands, but I hope it is also useful to PhD candidates, postdocs and people who have worked in Dutch mathematics for many years.

1. The Dutch academic research landscape

Universities and universities of applied sciences

Dutch higher education has two main types of institution: universities (universiteiten), offering wetenschappelijk onderwijs (WO, academic higher education), and universities of applied sciences (hogescholen), offering hoger beroepsonderwijs (HBO, higher professional education). Universities offer academic bachelor's and master's degrees, award doctorates and conduct research. Universities of applied sciences provide professionally oriented degrees and conduct practice-based research, often through research groups headed by a lector.

The Netherlands has 14 publicly funded universities. Delft, Eindhoven and Twente have a predominantly technical profile; Wageningen specialises in life sciences, agriculture and the environment; and the Open University focuses on distance education. The 14 universities are:

Academic positions at Dutch universities

University job titles in the Netherlands include promovendus (PhD candidate), postdoc (postdoctoral researcher), universitair docent (assistant professor, UD), universitair hoofddocent (associate professor, UHD) and hoogleraar (full professor). PhD candidates are usually employed by a university and paid a salary, although arrangements without an employment contract also exist (see my page on PhD positions in the Netherlands). Both PhD and postdoctoral positions are temporary. UD, UHD and professor positions usually combine research, teaching, supervision and administrative duties.

Another scientific staff position at Dutch universities is docent (lecturer or university teacher), with duties focused primarily or entirely on teaching, course development and supervision. Some universities employ lecturers on permanent contracts, as part of their scientific staff. The university job-classification system distinguishes several lecturer levels (docent 1 through docent 4). Some institutions also offer education-oriented careers at the UD, UHD or professor level. The exact teaching, research and management duties depend on the position and university.

University medical centres (UMCs)

The Netherlands has seven university medical centres (universitair medisch centrum, UMC). They combine specialised patient care, medical education and research. They are affiliated with universities, although not every university has a UMC. Researchers in medical statistics, epidemiology and clinical methodology may be employed directly by a UMC or by a connected university.

What are business schools?

A business school specialises in management, business administration, finance and related subjects. Some are schools within a university, such as Rotterdam School of Management at Erasmus University Rotterdam. TIAS School for Business and Society is associated with Tilburg University and Eindhoven University of Technology. Nyenrode Business Universiteit is a private university with accredited academic programmes.

Business schools and faculties of economics also employ researchers in econometrics, statistics, operations research, optimisation and analytics. In the Netherlands, substantial parts of econometrics research are carried out outside mathematics departments.

NWO: a research funder, and NWO-I: research institutes

NWO (the Dutch Research Council) is the main national organisation funding scientific research. It runs research programmes and grants across disciplines and supports national research infrastructure. I discuss grant schemes separately on my funding page.

NWO-I is the Institutes Organisation of NWO. It comprises ten national research institutes, which employ researchers and support staff. The institutes have their own scientific programmes and collaborate extensively with universities.

CWI (Centrum Wiskunde & Informatica) in Amsterdam is the national research institute for mathematics and computer science. Its research spans probability, statistics, optimisation, algorithms and machine learning. Many senior CWI researchers hold part-time professorships at Dutch universities, often for one day a week. More than thirty CWI researchers currently have part-time appointments at ten Dutch universities, through which they also teach and supervise students. PhD candidates carry out their research at CWI, but receive their doctorate from a university. The degree is normally awarded by the university where their CWI supervisor has a part-time professorship. These appointments are an important connection between CWI and the university mathematics community. My own arrangement is the reverse of the usual pattern: I work four days a week at the University of Twente and one day at CWI.

KNAW and other research institutes

KNAW (the Royal Netherlands Academy of Arts and Sciences) is a national academy that advises on science and scientific policy and manages its own research institutes. These include NIOO-KNAW (ecology), the Netherlands Institute for Neuroscience, the Hubrecht Institute and NIDI (demography). The Hubrecht Institute, in Utrecht, conducts fundamental research in developmental and stem-cell biology, including organoids and regeneration, and is affiliated with UMC Utrecht. KNAW institutes employ researchers independently of university departments, although many researchers work closely with universities.

The Netherlands Cancer Institute (NKI) is another major research institute. It is closely connected with the Antoni van Leeuwenhoek hospital, but is not a UMC or an NWO-I institute.

The Young Academy and local Young Academies

De Jonge Akademie (The Young Academy) is an independent part of KNAW. Its fifty members are researchers from different disciplines who contribute to science policy, the improvement of the academic system, interdisciplinarity, science communication and the place of science in society. Members are appointed for five years, with ten new members selected each year. Candidates must normally have obtained their PhD within the preceding ten years, with extensions available for career interruptions.

Many universities and UMCs have their own Young Academies. These are independent local groups, generally composed of early- and mid-career faculty from different disciplines. They participate in discussions on institutional policy, teaching, research conditions and academic culture, and organise events across faculties. Membership criteria vary between institutions.

2. The Dutch mathematics community

Mathematics in the Netherlands has an extensive national infrastructure. Researchers collaborate through societies, four mathematics clusters, shared education and national scientific meetings. It is common for PhD candidates and faculty to attend courses and seminars organised by other universities.

PWN and KWG

Platform Wiskunde Nederland (PWN) represents mathematics in the Netherlands. It works on research and education policy, national infrastructure and the position of mathematics in society, and provides a channel between the mathematical community, policymakers and research funders.

The Koninklijk Wiskundig Genootschap (KWG), the Royal Dutch Mathematical Society, was founded in 1778. It organises scientific events, supports mathematical education and publishes Nieuw Archief voor Wiskunde and Indagationes Mathematicae. Its Young KWG activities are directed at younger mathematicians.

The Netherlands Mathematical Congress (Nederlands Mathematisch Congres, NMC) brings together mathematicians from different fields for lectures, specialised sessions and other scientific activities. In 2026 the congress was held jointly with the Belgian and Luxembourg mathematical societies as the BeNeLux Mathematical Congress.

Mastermath: a national programme of mathematics courses

Mastermath is the national programme of advanced mathematics courses offered jointly by Dutch universities. Students can take courses taught by specialists at other institutions, including subjects that their own university does not offer regularly. Courses may involve travelling to another university, online teaching or a combination.

Mastermath is primarily for master's students, but PhD candidates can also participate in relevant courses, subject to the applicable arrangements. The course catalogue contains the courses offered in current and previous semesters.

Four national mathematics clusters

The four national mathematics clusters bring together research groups at universities and research institutes working in related areas of mathematics. They support collaboration across institutions and organise conferences, workshops, joint research activities and educational programmes.

  • DIAMANT — discrete mathematics, algorithms, algebra and number theory. This includes combinatorics, theoretical computer science and parts of discrete optimisation.

  • GQT — geometry and quantum theory, bringing together research in geometry and mathematical physics.

  • NDNS+ — nonlinear dynamics and related areas such as analysis, differential equations, scientific computing and continuous optimisation.

  • STAR stands for Stochastics – Theoretical and Applied Research. It is the national mathematics cluster for probability, mathematical statistics, stochastic processes and stochastic decision-making. It supports scientific meetings, thematic activities, collaborations and education, including work connected with Mastermath.

The clusters overlap. For example, mathematical decision-making connects DIAMANT, NDNS+ and STAR. See PWN's overview of the four clusters for their scope.

Further national mathematical initiatives

The 4TU Applied Mathematics Institute connects applied mathematics at the technical universities. The AI & Mathematics (AIM) initiative brings together researchers working on the mathematics of artificial intelligence.

Lorentz Center workshops

The Lorentz Center in Leiden organises international research workshops across all scientific disciplines, including mathematics and computer science. The workshops typically last five days and give small groups of researchers time for discussion and collaboration. Researchers can propose and organise workshops; accepted proposals receive organisational support. The workshop programme includes upcoming meetings.

Networks for inclusion, mentoring and community

Several Dutch networks provide mentoring, professional contacts and scientific meetings for groups underrepresented in mathematics and related fields.

  • European Women in Mathematics – The Netherlands (EWM-NL) is the Dutch branch of European Women in Mathematics. It organises meetings and workshops, promotes the visibility of women mathematicians and runs a mentoring network for women in mathematics. It is directly relevant to researchers across pure and applied mathematics, including statistics.

  • WIQD – We in Quantum Development is a Dutch professional network for underrepresented groups working in quantum science and technology, including academia, industry and policy. Founded as Women in Quantum Development by Julia Cramer and Stacey Jeffery (CWI, QuSoft and the University of Amsterdam), it broadened its name and remit in 2025. Although quantum technology is not mathematics in the narrow sense, this network is relevant to people in mathematical physics, theoretical computer science and quantum information.

  • QFIN – Queer and Feminist Informatics Network is based at the University of Amsterdam's Informatics Institute, with connections to CWI. It welcomes researchers, students and allies working in or interested in queer and feminist perspectives on computing, including AI and data science, and organises seminars, discussions and community events.

University-wide LGBTQ+ networks include UvA Pride and Th!nk with Pride at the University of Twente, which organises activities and provides information and support for LGBTQ+ staff and students.

3. Probability, statistics and operations research

The main national organisations in probability, statistics and operations research include the STAR cluster, the VVSOR scientific society, LNMB and EURANDOM. They organise complementary programmes of research meetings, courses, workshops and conferences.

STAR: the stochastics cluster

STAR stands for Stochastics – Theoretical and Applied Research. It connects Dutch researchers in probability, mathematical statistics, stochastic processes and stochastic optimisation or decision-making. STAR supports events, thematic activities, collaborations and education, including contributions to Mastermath. Upcoming events are announced on the STAR website and through the national mailing lists.

VVSOR: the society for statistics and operations research

VVSOR (Vereniging voor Statistiek en Operations Research) is the Netherlands Society for Statistics and Operations Research. It represents statisticians and operations researchers in academia, government, business and industry. It has sections for mathematical statistics, biometrics, operations research, statistics education, young statisticians and other subject areas.

The VVSOR Annual Meeting covers a wide range of statistics and operations research, including methodological developments and applications. VVSOR publishes the research journal Statistica Neerlandica and the society magazine STAtOR.

Biostatistics: BMS-ANed

BMS-ANed connects the biometrics section of VVSOR and the Netherlands region of the International Biometric Society. It organises scientific meetings in biostatistics and related fields and maintains a register of biostatisticians. Its activities cover medical statistics, clinical research, epidemiology and statistics in the life sciences.

LNMB: mathematics of operations research

LNMB (Landelijk Netwerk Mathematische Besliskunde) is a national network in the mathematics of operations research. It coordinates PhD courses and scientific activities in optimisation, scheduling, queues, stochastic networks and decision-making, involving researchers from mathematics and economics departments and CWI.

EURANDOM: workshops and visitors in stochastics

EURANDOM is a centre at Eindhoven University of Technology that organises international workshops and research activities in stochastics. Its scientific themes include spatial random structures, queueing and performance analysis, and statistical information modelling.

National seminars and recurring meetings

Several national seminars and recurring meetings bring together researchers from different Dutch institutions:

  • Van Dantzig Seminar — a national seminar in statistics, with speakers from the breadth of the statistical sciences. The venue changes between institutions, and it is supported by, among others, STAR and the mathematical statistics section of VVSOR.

  • Mark Kac Seminar — a longstanding seminar in Utrecht at the interface of probability and mathematical physics, covering topics such as interacting particle systems, random structures and statistical mechanics.

  • National Stochastics Meeting — the traditional national gathering of the stochastics community, historically associated with Lunteren. Announcements and practical details can be found via STAR and the Stoch-Ned mailing list.

  • Annual meeting of PhD candidates in stochastics — organised through the AiO Network Stochastics, combining short courses with talks by PhD candidates from across the Netherlands.

Universities also run local probability, statistics, econometrics and machine-learning seminars, many of which are open to visitors.

Mailing lists worth subscribing to

Seminar announcements, workshops, job vacancies and calls for participation circulate through several mailing lists:

  • Stoch-Ned (stoch-ned-l) — announcements for the Dutch stochastics community, including events, talks and academic positions. This is the list I would particularly recommend to researchers in probability and mathematical statistics.

  • Machine Learning Nederland — announcements for academic machine learning in the Netherlands, including seminars, meetings and positions. The list was initiated by Tim van Erven and has a public subscription page.

Newsletters from PWN, KWG and the sections of VVSOR cover additional activities.

4. Where can you work as a statistician and do research?

Statisticians in the Netherlands can carry out research in universities, medical centres, dedicated research institutes, public research organisations and private companies. Research positions differ considerably in their balance between statistical methodology, collaboration on applied projects, teaching and other duties.

Mathematics, statistics and computer-science departments

University departments in mathematics, applied mathematics, computer science, data science and related fields employ researchers working on mathematical statistics, probability, learning theory, stochastic processes and optimisation. CWI also has research groups in these areas.

University medical centres and other biomedical institutes

UMCs employ statisticians in biostatistics, epidemiology, clinical trials and medical research. Topics include survival analysis, prediction, causal inference, diagnostic testing and statistical methods for clinical studies. Several UMCs have substantial statistical research groups that work on methodology, including mathematically theoretical questions. Examples include Medical Statistics at LUMC, the Big Statistics group at Amsterdam UMC and the Biostatistics group at Radboudumc. All three offer opportunities for independent research in statistical methods, alongside collaborations with medical researchers. Other positions within UMCs may place greater emphasis on the design and analysis of clinical studies; the balance depends on the group and the position.

The Netherlands Cancer Institute (NKI) provides an important research environment in oncology. Wageningen University & Research has groups working on experimental design, biometry and statistical methods in the life sciences and agriculture.

Econometrics, economics and business schools

Econometrics and statistics are closely connected in the Netherlands, with research on time series, causal inference, forecasting, finance and quantitative decision-making. Relevant departments can be found at Erasmus University Rotterdam, Tilburg University, the University of Amsterdam, Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam, Maastricht University and the University of Groningen. Business schools may also recruit researchers in quantitative methods, analytics and operations research.

Public research organisations: CBS, RIVM and TNO

Three public research organisations employ statisticians and researchers in related fields:

  • CBS (Statistics Netherlands) produces official national statistics and undertakes methodological research in areas such as surveys, sampling, administrative data, privacy, statistical modelling and data integration. Some positions have a strong research and publication component.

  • RIVM (the National Institute for Public Health and the Environment) performs research and provides evidence for public policy, including epidemiological modelling, public health surveillance, risk assessment and environmental statistics.

  • TNO is an independent applied-research organisation working on topics including health, technology, industry, mobility, defence and energy. Researchers may publish scientific work alongside projects conducted with government, universities and companies.

Depending on the position, researchers may publish methodological work, develop statistical tools and contribute to projects with universities.

Industry

Pharmaceutical companies, research laboratories, technology firms and other private employers also offer research positions in statistics and machine learning. Opportunities for independent research and scientific publication depend strongly on the employer and position.

5. Especially for PhD candidates and postdocs

PhD candidates and postdocs can participate in many of the activities listed above. There are also organisations specifically representing or supporting researchers at these career stages.

Representation and early-career networks

The national and local Young Academies are primarily relevant at a later career stage, usually once researchers have an independent academic appointment.

  • Promovendi Netwerk Nederland (PNN) is the national organisation representing PhD candidates in the Netherlands. It works on issues such as employment conditions, supervision and the position of PhD candidates in national policy. There are also local PhD organisations at many universities; PNN provides information about those networks.

  • Young KWG is the early-career part of the Royal Dutch Mathematical Society, with meetings and professional-development activities for PhD candidates, postdocs and other young mathematicians.

  • AiO Network Stochastics brings together PhD candidates in Dutch stochastics and organises an annual meeting combining courses and research talks. AiO is an older Dutch term for a PhD researcher (assistent in opleiding), which still survives in the name of the network.

  • VVSOR Young Statisticians is one of the VVSOR sections and connects early-career people interested in statistics, including those working outside university mathematics departments.

Courses, seminars and meeting other researchers

For advanced courses in probability, statistics and related areas, see Mastermath and the LNMB PhD programme. The Van Dantzig Seminar, Mark Kac Seminar and the national cluster meetings are open opportunities to meet researchers outside one's own department.

The Stoch-Ned and Machine Learning Nederland mailing lists announce upcoming activities. The Netherlands Mathematical Congress and the VVSOR Annual Meeting cover the wider mathematical and statistical communities.

Looking for positions

AcademicTransfer lists academic vacancies at Dutch universities and research institutes, including PhD, postdoc, lecturer and faculty positions. It also retains many closed vacancy advertisements, which can be useful for finding groups that recruit in your area and seeing what experience they expect.
